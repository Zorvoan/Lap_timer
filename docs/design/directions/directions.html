A · Timing monitor

Motorsport pit-wall language. Condensed uppercase type, pure black, dense timing sheet. Colour follows the racing convention: purple overall best, green your best, yellow slower. The interaction that matters: the delta bar grows left (faster) or right (slower) from the centre, readable from the corner of the eye.
Tradeoff Feels like real racing and packs the most information; newcomers must learn the colour code (a legend is always shown).

B · Stage book

Rally roadbook language. Each track is a numbered stage with its real route shape, length and climb. Light paper for browsing, dark for driving. Editorial headline type, mono numbers. The interaction that matters: you and your best run move along the real elevation profile, so you see where you are and how far ahead.
Tradeoff Most distinctive and built from the tracks themselves; needs a light and a dark theme and more drawing work (profiles exist for every track once elevation is filled in).

C · Map first

The map is the app. Every track is drawn where it really is; the nearest one sits in a bottom sheet with Drive. While driving, the map follows you with the road ahead, and a pill shows the gap. After the run, the route is painted green where you gained and red where you lost.
Tradeoff Easiest to grasp (everyone knows map apps) and shows where to go; the map competes for attention while driving and needs map data, so the numbers are smaller.
